## 4. Visual language

- **Palette.** Near-black void plus steel/gunmetal product plus a single cool accent (navy to electric blue to teal). Warm colour appears only in skin, and briefly in the yellow robot and magenta neon of the field montage. The colour temperature arc runs cold void → blue tech → teal human → mixed real-world → white rest → black.
- **Product CG language.** Extreme macro, very shallow depth of field, hard specular rim lights tracing edges, a floating product (no surfaces) until the dock shot. Motion is slow and weighty, with ease-in/out on every move and nothing snapping. Parts *assemble*; they don't explode outward.
- **Human language.** Moody, low-key, one strong coloured key light per shot. Faces stay serious and focused, never smiling at camera. Eye macros used twice as punctuation. Workers are always mid-task, never posing.
- **Use-case proof.** One POV UI shot (remote expert call over real equipment). That single shot carries the whole software story.
- **Typography.** Minimal: a geometric/neo-grotesk sans (Inter/SF-like), regular weight, small (cap height ~2.5–3% of frame height), centred, white on black, plain fades and no motion. The logo is the same size class.
- **Transitions.** Mostly hard cuts on the beat. Exceptions: a ghost/double-exposure dissolve into the first human (shot 8), a dip-to-blue before the eye (14), two 0.2–0.4 s whip-blur flash bursts in the montage, and a hard cut to black before the titles.

## 6. Takeaways for our version

1. **Keep:** the five-act arc, macro CG assembly, a single cool accent colour, the drop at ~45% of runtime, the one bright "rest" shot before black, and minimal end cards.
2. **Change:** the reference shows **zero specs on screen**, and our brief needs specs shown. Add restrained, Apple-style spec callouts (thin line plus small type) on the CG assembly shots, **one spec per shot**, timed to the part that proves it. Text should feel like part of the film's quiet confidence, not a slideshow.
3. **Do not copy:** RealWear's industrial design (curved band with drop-down boom pod and display), their logo, "Arc", "Ari OS", or their dock shape. Our product must be visually distinct; "similar" applies to the filmmaking, not the object.

### 7.2 Things §3 under-describes
- Shot 1 is not a static lens rim: the camera pushes slowly while a specular band *travels* across the rim (fast layer ≈ 106 px/s at 1080p while the body drifts ≈ 4 px/s). The "glide" is the whole event. Our shot 1 (contact tip) needs the same two-speed motion: slow body, fast highlight.
- Shot 2 (prism) is the fastest Act 1 shot (≈ 60 px/s median): a tumble, not a drift. Shot 3 (rings) is nearly static (≈ 1.5 px/s). Act 1 therefore alternates slow / fast / slow / medium. Keep that alternation.
- Shot 9 (hero reveal) is cut on the beat *before* the hands shot, and it is the darkest shot in the film (mean luma 24, 68% near-black): the product is a thin rim-lit outline, not a lit object. Our hero must be equally dark.
- Shot 16 (POV UI): the UI window is red-tinted and sits in the right third; the real-world cabinet stays sharp. There is no outline or tracking in the reference; the window simply floats. Ours tracks the part, which is harder, hence the steady-hands requirement.
- Shot 25 (dock): the hand enters from top-right, seats the headset, lifts out of frame in ~0.6 s, then the product holds alone for ~1 s. The "exhale" is that hold. Our shot 27 keeps the hold.
- Title and logo are tiny: cap height ≈ 3% of frame height, Regular weight, pure white on pure black (mean luma 0.6 and 3.3). Title card is centred; the logo card is a glyph + wordmark at the same size class.
